## Verification

- Values observed on Deephaven 42.9 (started from `oracle/bin/start-java.sh` on JDK 21) for
  each filter test added: hexadecimal literals on `double` and `float` columns (match, range,
  search), non-ASCII digits on `int`, `long`, `BigDecimal` and `BigInteger` columns, the
  case-insensitive matches of supplementary characters (sent over gRPC: jpy truncates code
  points above the Basic Multilingual Plane, so console scripts cannot carry them), searches
  for date times in named zones, and instant literals with zones and offsets.
- JDK 21 behaviour observed directly with a Java program: `Double.parseDouble` and
  `Float.parseFloat` of 276 hexadecimal texts (`java_hex.txt`), `Double.toString` of 907
  doubles below `1e-300` (`java_tiny_doubles.txt`), `Character.toUpperCase(int)` and
  `toLowerCase(int)` of every code point (`java_case.txt`), `Character.digit` of every
  `char`, `equalsIgnoreCase` of 29 pairs, and the `DecimalFormat` of 120,000 doubles
  (random over the whole range, from 2^53 up, near ties), all of which the port matched;
  1,981 of them, mostly those the shortest digits get wrong, are in `java_formats.txt`.
- Each new test was seen to fail with the behaviour broken: ties to odd and no subnormal
  rounding (hexadecimal), ASCII digits only, the rounding bit of the two-digit decimal
  dropped, supplementary characters mapped as their low sixteen bits, the formatter's
  `rounded_up` flag ignored, a lower case `t` accepted by the precision pattern; and the
  tests of L3, L2, C1, L1, L4 fail on the code before this task (it refused them).
- Two branches of the ported conversion are read from the source but no sample reached
  them: a mutation of each survives the fixtures (the exact-arithmetic `high` test written
  `>` for `>=`, and the extra digit forced below `1e-3` and from `1e8`).
