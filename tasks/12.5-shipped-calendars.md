## Scope

Owner's answer to item 7.9 (task 11.1): "Write all three ourselves". Deephaven 42.9 starts with three calendars,
`UTC` (the default, `Calendar.default`), `USNYSE_EXAMPLE` and `USBANK_EXAMPLE`. Their files are under the Deephaven
Community License, so they are not copied, translated or reworded. Write our own three in the calendar file format
the reader already parses, from public sources, load them at start, and serve what task 11.1 refused for lack of
them.

## Notes

### What is shipped

`crates/oxhaven-expr/src/calendar/shipped/generate.py` (standard library only) writes the three files from rules,
and cites the source of each rule in a comment; `--check` fails when a file is not what it writes. The files are
compiled into the binary (`include_str!`) and read when the collection is first used, with the zone aliases
Deephaven ships, as Java reads them when its server starts (`Calendars#calendarsFromConfiguration`). `UTC` is the
default.

| Calendar | Zone | Valid dates | Hours | Weekend | Holidays and early closes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `UTC` | `UTC` | 1900-01-01 to 2100-12-31 | 00:00 to 23:59:59.999999999, end included | none | none |
| `USNYSE_EXAMPLE` | `America/New_York` | 1999-01-01 to 2027-12-31 | 09:30 to 16:00 | Saturday, Sunday | NYSE Rule 7.2 holidays with their observance rules, Juneteenth from 2022, MLK Day from 1998; the unscheduled closings (11 to 14 September 2001, 11 June 2004, 2 January 2007, 29 and 30 October 2012, 5 December 2018, 9 January 2025); 1:00 p.m. early closes on the day after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve on a Monday to Thursday, 3 July on a Monday, Tuesday or Thursday (and on a Wednesday from 2013), 5 July 2002, 26 December 2003 |
| `USBANK_EXAMPLE` | `America/New_York` | 2013-01-01 to 2027-12-31 | 09:00 to 17:00 | Saturday, Sunday | The legal public holidays of 5 U.S.C. 6103(a), observed as the Federal Reserve Banks observe them (a Sunday holiday on the Monday after, a Saturday holiday not at all), Juneteenth from 2022; the holidays of 1999 to 2012 are listed too, before the valid range, as Deephaven's file lists them (`holidays()` shows them) |

The names, the default, the zones, hours, weekends and valid ranges are those of Deephaven's calendars (facts a
user observes). `UTC`'s description is `UTC`, the same one word as Deephaven's, so its `toString` is the same;
the descriptions of the other two are our own text and differ from Deephaven's.

### Compared with Deephaven 42.9

Both servers ran the same script (`tasks/12.5-shipped-calendars/probe_server.py`, through `probe_client.py`) on
the three calendars: for every date each covers (110,000 dates), whether it is a business day and its business
hours as instants; and 400 samples per calendar at random dates and instants of 18 functions (number of business
and non-business dates with both inclusivities, next and previous business day, plus and minus business and
non-business days of dates and instants, fraction of a standard day, last business day of the month and week,
business time, fraction of the day complete and remaining, business nanoseconds, days and years between two
instants). `probe_figure.py` read each calendar as plotly-express's `FigureCalendar` does, and `probe_px.py` made
`dx.line(..., calendar=...)` with `calendar=True`, `'UTC'`, `'USNYSE_EXAMPLE'` and `'USBANK_EXAMPLE'` with
`deephaven-plugin-plotly-express` 0.22.0 on both servers and compared what each figure sends.

Results:

- `UTC`: identical, every date, every sample, `toString`, the figure.
- Every other difference is one of the dates below. Of the 21,600 sample answers (1,200 samples of 18
  functions), 463 differ, and each has a date below within its reach (`explain.py`); one more differs only in
  the stack trace Java appends to the text of an error.
- The figures: the JSON is identical but for the calendar's holidays, which differ exactly on the dates below.

`shipped_tests.rs` holds the comparison as unit tests: the per-year business days, business nanoseconds and a hash
of every day's hours, the holiday list, and 40 of the samples per calendar, recorded from Deephaven
(`record.py`), each checked with the differences below put back.

One date where the rules first disagreed with Deephaven was decided for Deephaven by the public record: 26 December
2003, a 1:00 p.m. close announced by the NYSE (Mondo Visione, "NYSE to close early on December 26", and UPI, 26
December 2003), added to `generate.py` as an announced early close.

### What is now served

- `calendar()`, `calendar("UTC")`, `calendar("USNYSE_EXAMPLE")`, `calendar("USBANK_EXAMPLE")` in any case, and
  every method of them; `calendar_names()` lists them as before.
- A plotly-express figure with `calendar=True` (the default calendar, `UTC`) or a shipped calendar's name: the two
  recorded figures with `calendar=` that tasks 5.8 and 11.1 left failing.
- Removing a shipped calendar and adding another of its name, as in Java.

Not part of this task, still refused by name: `StaticCalendarMethods` in formulas (`isBusinessDay(...)` and the
rest, about a hundred typed overloads; task 11.1's follow-up, no longer blocked on a default calendar) and
`Figure#businessTime` of `deephaven.plot`.
