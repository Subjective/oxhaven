## Scope

The cases the second end-to-end measurement still found slower than Deephaven in both runs,
in this order, each measured inside the engine before it is changed:

1. `aj`: 2x slower at 1M rows, 3x at 10M. Number the exact-match keys with the shared key
   table a chunk at a time, keep stamps typed in flat arrays, search on typed values.
2. `last_by`: 2x to 2.7x slower. A cheaper form for static and add-only parents.
3. `ungroup` at 10M rows: 9 ms against 3 to 4. Cause not known.
4. Low- and medium-cardinality aggregations at 10M rows (1.2x to 1.5x). Cause not known.
5. An "After task 9.9" block in `docs/BENCHMARK.md` with engine-example numbers.

Results, row order, row keys, error text and incremental behaviour do not change.

### Item 1: the as-of join

Measured with a new example, `examples/asof-cases.rs` (the benchmark's `aj` shape and six
variations, two of them refreshing). Under callgrind (200,000 left rows, `aj_strict`) the
join's instructions were 32% `BTreeSet::insert` of every left and right row into its
bucket, 18% `Plan::matched` (a `BTreeSet` range per left row), 16% `BTreeMap<Vec<Cell>,
Bucket>::entry` (a heap-allocated key per row), and most of the rest reading those keys.

What changed (`ops/asof/join.rs`, `stamps.rs`):

- Exact-match keys are numbered by `ops::key_table::KeyTable` (`KeyTable::new()`) a chunk
  at a time, and what the join keeps about a key is in vectors indexed by its slot. When a
  pair of exact-match columns has two Arrow types the table keeps apart (two object classes,
  such as a `LocalDate` and a `String`, or a `Boolean` and a `byte` among several columns;
  Java reads all of these as objects or as bytes and the join did not refuse them), the
  keys are still numbered as before, a `Vec<Cell>` per row in an ordered map, so that the
  results of those joins do not change. One column of 64 bits or fewer on each side always
  takes the table (its words compare by value, as the cells did).
- Stamps are typed (`StampValue`): a column of 64 bits or fewer is an `Option<i64>` per
  row, a `double` or `float` as its bits in an order that is `total_cmp`'s after NaN and
  `-0.0` are made one value; strings and `ZonedDateTime` keep the `Stamp` enum.
- A static right table's rows are one flat array grouped by slot and sorted by stamp and
  row key once; a left row's match is a partition point found by galloping from where the
  previous left row of its slot ended (a merge when left stamps are in order, a binary
  search otherwise). A refreshing right table keeps an ordered set per slot as before.
- The left rows of a key are kept only when the right table is refreshing: only a change
  of the right table looks them up. The initial redirection is made at once
  (`WritableRowRedirection::from_rows`), and the initial pass reads the left table 65,536
  rows at a time.
- Keys are never dropped: Java keeps every key, and the old code dropped an empty bucket
  only to save memory (what a bucket remembers mattered only when it was kept).

Result: `aj` 449 to 17 ms at 1M rows, 4,982 to 230 ms at 10M (Deephaven 1.7 s);
`aj_unordered_stamps` 15.0 to 2.3 s at 10M.

Tests: every as-of test passes unchanged, with `OXHAVEN_TEST_SEEDS=full` (unit, corpus,
the random families with the consistency checker). New: `tests::static_joins_match_a_look_at_every_right_row`
(random static tables against a brute force over every right row: four rules, long,
double and string stamps with nulls, NaN, both zeros and ties, left stamps in and out of
order, no key, an int key, a `Boolean` against a `byte` key, and a `Boolean` and an int
against a `byte` and an int, the last taking the cell path); `stamps::tests::words_order_as_stamps`;
`join::tests::a_partition_found_from_any_hint_is_the_partition`. Seen to fail: without
the sign flip of a negative double's word (2 tests); without sorting a group of right
stamps (19); with the key table for every pair of key types (the new brute-force test);
without the search below the hint (17).

### Item 2: `last_by`

Under callgrind (1M rows, 10,000 keys, six columns) the aggregation was about 570M
instructions per call: 234M in the six operators' `add_runs`, 170M in the framework noting
one effect per group and operator, 99M grouping each chunk's rows by slot, 44M numbering
keys. With 10,000 keys in chunks of 4,096 rows nearly every group of a chunk has one row.

What changed: an operator may say it takes added rows one at a time in row order without
their values (`AggOperator::adds_rows`/`add_rows`). `FirstLastCell` in `ByRowKey` mode (a
static or add-only parent) does: a row takes its group's place when its row key is beyond
the one kept, which over a chunk's rows comes to what the group's first or last row of them
does. When every operator of an aggregation takes rows so and no group rows are kept,
`Aggregator::process` does not group a chunk and the operators write their marks
(`operator::Marks`, moved from `aggregator.rs`) directly. The comparison is kept, so an
add-only parent that adds rows below its last row is still right.

Result: 29.5 to 18.6 ms at 1M rows, 339 to 155 to 180 ms at 10M (Deephaven 160 to 183).
Tests: `first_last::tests::rows_one_at_a_time_are_their_groups_at_once` (both first and
last, rows of a group before and after those kept, against `add_runs` with the same marks).
Seen to fail: taking only a group's first row failed it and 5 existing aggregation tests
(corpus, fixtures, incremental, static, time types).

### Item 3: `ungroup`

Measured with a new `ungroup` case of `examples/aggregation-cases.rs` (a fresh `head` of
`group_by` of 10M rows by 10,000 keys, as the benchmark): 3.7 ms at 10M rows, 0.7 at 1M,
so a cost per element. `ungroup_read_vectors`, which only reads the grouped column of every
row, took 3.4 of the 3.5 ms: `ungroup` reads the vectors to learn their sizes, and the
aggregation's result column (`SlotSource`) answered with a `take`, a copy of all 10M
elements. A read of consecutive slots of one written array is now a slice of it, which
copies nothing: `ungroup` 3.5 to 0.2 ms. Test: `slot_source::tests::consecutive_values_are_read_without_a_copy`.
Seen to fail: slicing from the array's start failed it and 13 aggregation tests.

### Item 4: aggregations with few groups

Under callgrind (`count_by_low` and `sum_by_low`, 1M rows, 10 keys) grouping each chunk's
rows by slot (`aggregator::group`, a sort of 4,096 packed integers) was 127 instructions
per row, more than numbering the keys and the operators together. A chunk whose slots lie
in a range not much wider than the chunk (`high - low < 4 * rows + 1024`) is now grouped by
a counting sort, which gives the same order and runs. At 10M rows, two interleaved rounds:
`sum_by_low` 179 to 190 to 109 to 113 ms, `avg_by_low` 190 to 195 to 107 to 112,
`count_by_low` 148 to 154 to 67 to 72, `sum_by_med` 369 to 385 to 293, `count_by_med` 158
to 166 to 133 to 151; `avg_by_med` 426 to 428 to 373 to 424 (no clear change). Test:
`aggregator::grouping_tests::counting_and_sorting_group_alike`. Seen to fail: runs cut
short in the counting sort failed it and many aggregation tests.

What is left with medium cardinality (10,000 keys): nearly one row per group per chunk, so
the per-group work of the operators (`add_runs`) and of the framework's marks is most of
it, as it was for `last_by`. A row-order path for operators that read values (sum, avg)
would need values in `add_rows`; not done. Splitting the input over threads
(`map_pieces`) was not tried.
